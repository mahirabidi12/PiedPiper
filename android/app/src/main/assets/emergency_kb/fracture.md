# Fracture First Aid

## Signs of a Fracture
- Visible deformity or unnatural angle at the injury site
- Severe pain, especially when moving or pressing on the area
- Swelling, bruising, or tenderness
- Inability or unwillingness to use the limb
- Grating sensation or sound (crepitus) when moved

## Immediate Steps
1. Keep the person calm and still — do not attempt to straighten or realign the bone.
2. Immobilize the injured area in the position you find it. Use whatever is available: boards, sticks, rolled clothing, belts.
3. Extend the splint past the joints above and below the fracture site.
4. Pad between the splint and the skin with cloth to prevent pressure sores.
5. Secure with strips of cloth, ties, or belts — firm but not so tight as to cut off circulation.
6. Elevate the limb if possible and no spinal injury is suspected.
7. Apply a cold pack or wet cloth wrapped in fabric to reduce swelling (20 min on, 20 min off).

## Open (Compound) Fracture
- A bone is protruding through the skin or a wound is visible near the fracture.
- Do NOT push the bone back in. Do NOT remove impaled objects.
- Cover the wound loosely with clean cloth to protect from contamination.
- Control bleeding with gentle pressure around (not on) the wound.
- Risk of infection is high — treat as critical.

## Spinal Fracture Warning
- If the injury involves the neck, back, or result of a fall/impact from height, assume spinal injury.
- Do NOT move the person unless they are in immediate danger.
- Hold the head and neck still if transport is unavoidable.

## Circulation Check (Every 15 Min)
- Press a fingernail beyond the splint until it blanches white, then release.
- Normal: colour returns within 2 seconds.
- If slow return, tingling, numbness, or blue skin: loosen the splint immediately.

## When to Treat as Critical
- Femur (thigh) fracture — can cause life-threatening internal blood loss
- Pelvis fracture
- Signs of shock: pale, cold, clammy skin; rapid weak pulse; confusion
- Open fracture with heavy bleeding
