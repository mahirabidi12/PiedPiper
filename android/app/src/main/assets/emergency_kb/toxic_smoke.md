# Toxic Smoke and Inhalation Injury

## Why It Kills
In structure fires, smoke inhalation kills more people than burns. Toxic gases include carbon monoxide (CO), hydrogen cyanide (HCN), and ammonia. CO is colourless and odourless — you cannot detect it without instruments.

## Immediate Actions
1. Get the person (and yourself) out of the smoke-filled area immediately.
2. Move to fresh air — outdoors or an area confirmed clear of smoke.
3. Keep the person in a semi-seated position if they are conscious and breathing. Lying flat can worsen breathing distress.
4. Loosen tight clothing around the neck and chest.
5. Keep the person calm and as still as possible — exertion increases oxygen demand.

## Signs of Inhalation Injury
- Singed nose hair, eyebrows, or facial hair
- Soot in the mouth or nose
- Hoarse voice, stridor (high-pitched sound when breathing in), or barking cough
- Burns on the face, lips, or inside the mouth
- Difficulty breathing, chest tightness
- Coughing up black or grey sputum

## Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Signs
- Headache, dizziness, confusion, weakness
- Cherry-red or pale/grey skin (unreliable sign)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Loss of consciousness
The only treatment in the field is fresh air. If available, high-flow oxygen via mask.

## Airway Warning
Upper airway swelling from inhalation can close the airway within hours. If any signs of inhalation injury are present, this is a critical emergency — even if the person seems stable now.

## Artificial Respiration
- If not breathing: tilt head back, lift chin, and give rescue breaths.
- If unconscious with no detectable pulse: begin CPR.

## Confined Space Rescue
- Never enter a smoke-filled or confined space without respiratory protection.
- Rescuers overcome by fumes become additional casualties. Signal the mesh first.
