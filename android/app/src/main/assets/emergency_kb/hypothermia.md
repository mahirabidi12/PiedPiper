# Hypothermia First Aid

## Definition
Hypothermia occurs when core body temperature drops below 35°C (95°F). It is life-threatening below 32°C (90°F).

## Stages and Signs

### Mild (32–35°C)
- Shivering, pale skin, slurred speech, clumsiness, confusion
- Person is still alert

### Moderate (28–32°C)
- Shivering stops (paradoxically — this is a danger sign)
- Severe confusion or drowsiness, muscle stiffness
- Slow pulse and breathing

### Severe (Below 28°C)
- Unconscious, no visible breathing, very weak or absent pulse
- Skin may appear blue or grey

## Immediate Steps
1. Move the person out of the cold environment. Shield from wind and wet.
2. Remove wet clothing carefully — rough handling can trigger cardiac arrest in severe cases.
3. Insulate from the ground (cardboard, sleeping bags, blankets, clothing).
4. Cover the head — substantial heat loss occurs there.
5. Apply warm (not hot) compresses to the neck, armpits, and groin — these are where blood vessels run close to the surface.
6. Do NOT rub the limbs — this drives cold blood from extremities to the core and can worsen cooling.
7. Do NOT give alcohol — it dilates blood vessels and increases heat loss.

## If Conscious and Can Swallow
- Give warm sweet drinks (not alcohol) in small sips.
- High-calorie food if available.

## If Unconscious or Pulseless
- Check for breathing and pulse for at least 60 seconds — in severe hypothermia, both can be too slow to detect quickly.
- If no pulse after 60 seconds: begin CPR.
- Do not assume death — "not dead until warm and dead." Continue resuscitation during rewarming.

## Rewarming Caution
- Rewarm slowly and gently. Rapid rewarming of limbs before the core is dangerous.
- The priority is core rewarming (torso and neck area first).
- In field conditions, skin-to-skin contact in a shared sleeping bag is effective for mild-to-moderate cases.
