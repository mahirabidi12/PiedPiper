# Emergency Water Purification

## Why This Matters
Contaminated water causes cholera, typhoid, dysentery, and Giardia. In a disaster, tap water may be unsafe within hours. Symptoms of waterborne illness (diarrhoea, vomiting) accelerate dehydration and kill.

## Step 1 — Filter Sediment First
Before disinfecting, remove large particles that protect pathogens from treatment.
- Strain through several layers of clean cloth, a coffee filter, or sand packed into a container.
- The clearer the water, the more effective disinfection will be.

## Step 2 — Boiling (Most Reliable Method)
- Bring water to a rolling boil for at least 1 minute (3 minutes above 2,000 m altitude).
- Allow to cool naturally in a clean covered container.
- Boiling kills bacteria, viruses, and protozoa (including Cryptosporidium and Giardia).

## Step 3 — Chemical Disinfection (When Boiling is Impossible)

### Unscented household bleach (sodium hypochlorite 5–8.25%):
- Clear water: 8 drops per litre (1/8 teaspoon per 4 litres). Mix and wait 30 minutes.
- Cloudy water: 16 drops per litre. Mix and wait 60 minutes.
- Water should smell faintly of chlorine. If no smell, repeat dose and wait 15 more minutes.

### Water purification tablets (iodine or chlorine):
- Follow package instructions precisely.
- Iodine is less effective against Cryptosporidium. Boiling is still preferred.

## What Bleach Does NOT Treat
- Heavy metals (lead, arsenic) — no field solution
- Chemical contamination — no field solution
- Cryptosporidium — requires boiling or a 0.1 micron filter

## Storage
- Store purified water in clean, covered containers.
- Avoid touching the inside of the container or the water with bare hands.
- Use within 24 hours; re-purify if contamination is suspected.

## Emergency Minimum
A human needs approximately 2.5–3 litres of water per day to survive in normal conditions. More in heat, after injury, or with illness. Prioritise water collection before thirst becomes severe.
