# Severe Bleeding First Aid

## Immediate Priority
Severe blood loss kills within minutes. Stop the bleeding before anything else.

## Steps to Control Bleeding
1. Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound with the cleanest material available — cloth, clothing, bandage.
2. Press hard and do not lift the material to check — this disrupts clotting.
3. If blood soaks through, add more material on top. Do not remove the original layer.
4. If the wound is on a limb and pressure alone fails, apply a tourniquet (see below).
5. Elevate the injured limb above heart level if no fracture or spinal injury is suspected.

## Tourniquet Application (Limbs Only)
- Use only when direct pressure fails to stop life-threatening limb bleeding.
- Apply 5–7 cm above the wound (not over a joint).
- Use a wide band (belt, folded cloth, cravat) — never wire, rope, or cord.
- Tighten until bleeding stops completely. Note the time of application.
- Do not remove once applied — this is a medical decision.

## Impaled Objects
- Do NOT remove impaled objects — they may be plugging the wound.
- Build padding around the object to stabilize it in place.
- Control bleeding around the object with gentle pressure.

## Internal Bleeding (No External Wound)
- Suspected when the person has: abdominal pain and rigidity, bruising over the abdomen or flanks, blood in urine/stool/vomit, signs of shock with no visible bleeding.
- There is nothing field-treatable. Keep the person still, warm, and lying down.
- Treat shock: lay flat, elevate legs 30 cm if no spinal or lower limb injury.

## Shock Recognition and Response
Pale, cold, clammy skin — rapid weak pulse — confusion or anxiety — rapid shallow breathing.
1. Lay the person flat.
2. Elevate the legs about 30 cm (unless head, spinal, or leg injury).
3. Keep warm with blankets or clothing.
4. Do not give food or water.
5. Maintain airway — turn on side if vomiting.
