# Burn First Aid

## Burn Severity Classification

### Minor (Superficial / 1st Degree)
- Red, dry, painful skin — like a sunburn.
- Treat at site: cool water, monitor.

### Moderate (Partial Thickness / 2nd Degree)
- Blisters, wet shiny skin, intense pain.
- Requires careful field treatment and urgent evacuation.

### Critical (Full Thickness / 3rd Degree)
- Waxy, leathery, charred, or white skin. Little or no pain (nerve destruction).
- Treat as a medical emergency.

## Immediate Steps (All Burns)
1. Remove the person from the heat source. Ensure your own safety first.
2. Cool the burn under cool (not cold, not icy) running water for at least 10–20 minutes.
   - Do NOT use ice, butter, toothpaste, or any other substance.
   - Do NOT break blisters.
3. Remove jewellery, watches, or tight clothing near the burn — swelling will trap them.
4. Cover loosely with clean non-fluffy material (cling film, a clean plastic bag, or wet cloth).
   - Do NOT use cotton wool or anything that will stick to the wound.

## Chemical Burns
1. Remove contaminated clothing while protecting yourself (gloves if available).
2. Flush continuously with large volumes of water for 20–30 minutes.
3. Do not attempt to neutralize the chemical with another substance.

## Electrical Burns
- Surface damage underestimates internal injury — electrical burns can track through tissue.
- Check for entry and exit wounds.
- Monitor for cardiac arrhythmia symptoms: irregular pulse, loss of consciousness.
- Treat any entry/exit wound as a burn.

## When to Treat as Critical
- Burns to face, hands, feet, genitals, or major joints
- Circumferential burns (wrapping all the way around a limb — can cut off circulation)
- Inhalation burns: singed nose hair, hoarse voice, soot in mouth/nose — airway can swell shut
- Burns covering more than 10% of body surface area
- Any 3rd degree burn

## Fluid Loss Warning
Large burns cause massive fluid loss. Watch for: extreme thirst, decreased or no urination, dizziness. Give small sips of water only if the person is conscious and not vomiting.
